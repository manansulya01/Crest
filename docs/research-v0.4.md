# Crest Research V0.4

All numbers below come from experiment artifacts in `experiments/results/`
and generated tables in `experiments/tables/`. Nothing here is estimated or
rounded in the researcher's favour.

## Abstract

V0.4 asks whether *graded* recovery evidence — with an independent holdout
generator and distribution shifts — predicts recovery success on environments
that were never used to verify it. It also asks whether that grading can
expand autonomous action coverage under a fixed exposure budget without
raising unsafe admissions. Measured answer: the grading mechanism works as
designed on the measured families and attacks, and fixture-only evidence was
demonstrably unsafe in one measured configuration; however Crest cannot yet
*grant* generalized evidence cheaply, so generalized autonomous coverage
(GAC) remains 0 in the default policy until verification sample sizes grow.

## Research question

Can independently verified recoverability measurably expand autonomous
action coverage while realized/certified exposure stays inside the
constitutional budget?

## System model

A = action class, S = target scope, R = recovery program, V = verifier,
E = certified exposure, B = budget (E_max), Q = evidence quality (sample
sufficiency), G = evidence generality (fixture/family/general class).

## Evidence model

- `VERIFIED_FIXTURE`: pass on training-generator fixtures only.
- `VERIFIED_FAMILY`: pass on training fixtures *and* an independently
  constructed holdout generator, with sample size and Wilson upper bound
  below policy thresholds.
- `VERIFIED_GENERAL`: additionally passes >= 3 distribution shifts
  (parameter, structural, adversarial) on the holdout generator.
- `UNVERIFIED`: no holdout evidence, or *any observed failure*. V0.3
  classified failing runs as `VERIFIED_FIXTURE`; V0.4 removes that conflation
  because it made failing recovery programs admissible at
  `min_evidence_class: VERIFIED_FIXTURE`.

## Statistical model

The bounded quantity is the *observed recovery failure rate under the
evaluated distributions*. The bound is a one-sided Wilson upper confidence
bound; confidence is configurable (default 0.95). It is **not** a bound on
real-world harm probability. Zero observed failures over n cases still yield a
nonzero bound; therefore small n cannot produce strong classes.

## Experimental design

- Generators: training (`crest.fixtures.generate`, fixed-shape trees) and
  holdout (`crest.holdout_gen.build`, path-graph construction; structurally
  independent).
- Distributions: IN_DISTRIBUTION, PARAMETER_SHIFT, STRUCTURAL_SHIFT,
  ADVERSARIAL_SHIFT, COMPOSITION_SHIFT, OUT_OF_FAMILY.
- Baseline: fixture-only evidence (V0.3 policy). Arms: fixture-only, family,
  generalized.

### Canonical configuration

The reported artifacts are produced with one declared configuration,
`experiments/configs/experiment.yaml`, loaded through
`experiments/generalization/config.py`. Experiment defaults, the CLI, and
the table-provenance checks resolve their parameters from that loader, so
the reproduction pipeline cannot silently run at different parameters than
the reported ones. (The V0.5 audit found exactly that defect: the CLI's
full reproduction defaulted to n=10 while the reported ablation was
generated at n=20. A later audit found a second instance: the CLI passed one
`--samples` value to every arm, so a canonical n=20 run silently ran
confidence at n=20 instead of 6 and cross-distribution at 20 instead of 8.
Canonical `--full` now passes no overrides and lets each experiment resolve
its own value from this file.)

| experiment | canonical parameter | value |
|---|---|---|
| ablations | n per measurement | 20 |
| cross-distribution | samples per distribution | 8 |
| confidence sensitivity | n per measurement | 6, confidence levels 0.90/0.95/0.99/0.999 |
| exposure sweep | E_max values | 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 |
| drift | cases per class | 3 (8 classes -> 24 outcomes) |

The ablation and confidence arms classify under an *experimental* policy
scaled to n (minimum verification/holdout cases = n, minimum total samples
= 2n, failure upper bound 0.20); this is deliberately looser than the
production policy documented in the same file (family bound 0.05, general
bound 0.01), which remains the runtime default. Canonical is a provenance
statement — these are the parameters the committed tables were produced
with — not a claim that they are statistically optimal. Sample counts (and,
where used, seeds) are recorded per run in the JSONL provenance records;
seeds are fixed constants.

The committed `experiments/tables/*` files were regenerated by the
canonical `--full` run on 2026-10-21 and now carry the provenance header.
Their scientific cells are identical to the previously committed tables
(verified after normalizing the timing columns and provenance header); the
only changes are the provenance header and new wall-clock timing values.
Exact table bytes are therefore not portable across runs or machines (see
"Known nondeterminism" and `docs/final-autonomous-audit.md` §28).

## Results (measured)

Cross-distribution (`experiments/tables/cross_distribution.md`, n=8): general
recovery `restore_from_snapshot` scored 1.00 on all eight distributions;
`overfit_distractor_gate` scored 1.00 in-distribution on the tree family but
0.00 on the independent holdout generator, and 1.00 only on the adversarial
shift (which re-injects the artifacts it keys on). `noop_fault` scored 0.00
everywhere.

Ablation at n=20 (`experiments/tables/ablations.md`):

| mode | honest recovery class | overfit recovery | false admissions | false rejections | mean cost |
|------|------------------------|------------------|------------------|------------------|-----------|
| fixture_only | VERIFIED_FIXTURE | admitted (2 cases) | 2 | 0 | 6.59 s |
| family_evidence | VERIFIED_FAMILY | UNVERIFIED | 0 | 0 | 13.59 s |
| generalized_evidence | VERIFIED_GENERAL | UNVERIFIED | 0 | 0 | 12.49 s |

The costs are the mean of all eight rows per mode in the 2026-10-21
canonical run; they are wall-clock measurements and will differ on another
run or machine.

Exposure sweep (`exposure_sweep.md`): AAC rises 0.0 -> 0.5 -> 0.5 -> 1.0 for
E_max = 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0; certified exposure never exceeded the budget;
GAC stayed 0 because the sweep's small verification samples could not satisfy
the family thresholds — conservatism, not a bug.

Confidence sensitivity (n=6, 90/95/99/99.9%): identical admissions in all
four settings, because decisions are dominated by observed failures rather
than by the confidence parameter at these sample sizes.

The measured coverage/unsafe-admission/cost frontier is visualized in
`experiments/plots/frontier.png` (script:
`experiments/generalization/frontier_plot.py`); default experiment
parameters are recorded in `experiments/configs/experiment.yaml`.

Drift (`drift.md`): only evidence-family nesting and target identity are
implemented. Unpinned rename/move drift is NOT detected (documented
limitation; target pinning was added to close it for pinned capabilities).
Content drift, scope drift, symlink topology and git-state drift remain
unimplemented and are reported as such.

Composition (`crest.composition`): single-step and modify-then-move sequences
verified with full-snapshot recovery; composition is not assumed anywhere.

## Reproducing this document

From the repository root with the source tree importable:

```powershell
$env:PYTHONPATH="src"                            # plus the repository root for `experiments`
crest research reproduce                         # quick: verify the stored results; writes no artifacts
crest research reproduce --full                  # canonical: run experiments at canonical parameters,
                                                 # evaluate the conclusion, regenerate experiments/tables/*
crest research reproduce --full --samples 4      # explicit override: n=4, marked non-canonical,
                                                 # never replaces the canonical tables
```

Semantics:

- Quick mode evaluates the conclusion from the stored JSONL results and
  records their hashes and recorded configuration in the manifest. It never
  writes research artifacts and cannot be mistaken for a canonical run: the
  manifest records the canonical configuration, while
  `conclusion_details.data_config` records the sample count actually found
  in the stored data (20 after a canonical full run; an older or overridden
  stored file will report its own value).
- Full mode runs every experiment first, then the conclusion, then the
  tables, so the artifacts describe the results of that same run. (Before
  this was fixed, `--full` regenerated tables from the *previous* run's
  results and then overwrote those results.)
- `--samples` is valid only with `--full`. It overrides the sample count for
  the ablations, cross-distribution, and confidence arms; exposure budgets
  and drift cases stay at their canonical values. Any explicit override is
  marked non-canonical, even if it equals the canonical value; canonical
  evidence requires the declared configuration rather than a command line
  that mimics it.
- Tables are regenerated only by a canonical full run whose experiments all
  succeeded. Reduced/overridden runs and failed runs skip table regeneration
  and say so in the manifest. `crest experiment tables` refuses to
  regenerate canonical tables from non-canonical stored results unless
  `--allow-noncanonical` is passed explicitly.
- Table replacement is validated and staged: every table is rendered and
  validated in memory, staged in a temporary directory under the target,
  and only then moved into place; generation or validation failures leave
  the previous artifacts untouched, and a replacement failure rolls back
  files already replaced. Generated Markdown tables carry a provenance
  comment with the schema version, the canonical configuration digest, and
  the sha256 of each source JSONL file.
- `crest experiment <name>` runs a single experiment at its canonical
  parameters; per-module `--n` / `--cases` flags override explicitly.

What a reproduction manifest records (new file per run under
`reproduce_manifests/`, ignored by git):

- `experiment_config`: canonical vs CLI override, sample count, canonical
  flag, failure bound, and the configuration digest (sha256 over the
  normalized canonical parameters — comments and formatting do not change
  it; any parameter that affects the experiments does).
- `sources`: raw sha256 and size of each result file the conclusion and
  tables consumed, plus its status.
- `scientific_sources`: normalized sha256 of each result file, excluding
  run timestamps, timing/latency observations, and machine/code provenance
  fields. These are the source digests used by `result_digest`.
- `config`: sha256 of the runtime `crest.yaml` selected by the CLI.
- `git_commit`, `working_tree_dirty`, tool versions, and run timestamps.
- `artifacts`: raw sha256 and size for every written table (hashed from the
  bytes actually staged), plus `content_sha256`, a normalized table hash that
  removes the provenance header and timing/latency cells.
- `result_digest` (manifest schema 3): sha256 over the scientific content
  only — verdict, reason, criteria (excluding `C6_general_cost_mean_seconds`
  and `C7_fixture_cost_mean_seconds`), `data_config`, experiment
  configuration, normalized source digests, normalized table content hashes,
  experiment statuses, and failures. It changes when a parameter, input,
  classification, count, or failure changes. It does not cover raw bytes,
  timestamps, timing values, or machine metadata.
- `performance_digest`: separate sha256 over the observational record —
  run duration, C6/C7, per-source timing/latency values, raw source hashes,
  and raw artifact hashes/sizes. It is expected to change on every run.
- Timestamps are recorded in the manifest but are not part of either digest.

Known nondeterminism: row-level `verification_seconds` and drift latencies
are wall-clock measurements, so raw JSONL/table bytes and
`performance_digest` differ across runs. `result_digest` is stable across
repeated canonical full runs with identical scientific results: the
2026-10-21 release-gate runs produced
`38e71b6d67e4ead7dfdf55c63af0ae9f8da05ec115c7fb7ad259525f3e998905` twice,
while their `performance_digest` values and raw table hashes differed.
Classification columns, sample counts, normalized source digests, and
normalized table content hashes are deterministic (fixed seeds).

## Baselines

Uncontrolled and self-grading arms from Milestone 2 are unchanged;
transactional rollback is not re-implemented in V0.4 (the composition
verifier is the closest analogue and it measures, not assumes).

## Metrics

AAC = fraction of eligible action classes currently permitted.
GAC = fraction permitted with evidence >= VERIFIED_FAMILY.
False admission = admitted under policy but evaluation success < 0.999 on
the independent distributions. False rejection = denied although evaluation
success >= 0.999. EvidenceEfficiency = coverage gain per verification second.

## Ablations

No-holdout (fixture-only) admits an unsafe recovery; family and generalized
policies reject it. Drift/reprobe ablations remain partially measured:
reprobe is exercised per filesystem execution, drift only for the classes
listed above.

## Limitations

- Two environment families; no database, container, or network families.
- Deterministic seeds, not probabilistic sampling.
- One malicious recovery family (fixture detection) plus Milestone 2 attacks.
- Verification cost grows with n; at n=20 generalized verification costs
  ~8 s per capability on this machine, still acceptable but not free.
- Receipts are content-addressed, not signed.

## Falsification criteria and outcome

Pre-declared criteria are implemented in
`experiments/generalization/conclusion.py`. On the measured data the verdict
is **SUPPORTED** within the measured families and attacks: generalized
evidence admitted the honest recovery, rejected the fixture-overfitting
recovery, and fixture-only evidence produced 2 measured false admissions.

The verdict is explicitly scoped: it says nothing about untested families,
real agent environments, or true harm.

## Conclusion

Within its measured scope, Crest can earn autonomy for genuinely general
recovery programs and refuse it to programs that only work on the fixture.
The open question — how much useful autonomy can be *granted* (GAC > 0)
without inordinate verification cost — is not yet answered.
