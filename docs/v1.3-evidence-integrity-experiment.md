## 1. Research question

Can CREST reason about the **integrity** of its evidence — not merely whether several systems agree — and use that understanding to make better, safer, more economical execution decisions?

## 2. Prior-art discoveries

See `research/EVIDENCE_INTEGRITY_PRIOR_ART.md`. Closest prior art: database provenance semirings (Green/Karvounarakis/Tannen, 2007), GSN assurance cases (Kelly & Weaver, 2004), common-cause Bayesian networks (Li et al.; Sun et al., 2021), LLM-as-judge reliability work (Zheng et al. 2023; Landesberg et al. 2025; JudgeBench), and distributed-systems quorum/BFT (Cowling et al. 2006; AAAI 2026 multi-agent reliability).

**Honest novelty assessment:** the ingredients are all published. What I could not find in a single published system is a **runtime execution gate that discounts verifier agreement by provenance-derived independence, refuses to amplify confidence under unknown dependency, and couples the resulting bound to a risk-weighted execution decision**. Classification: **new integration**, leaning toward **new experimentally demonstrated capability** only if the benchmark shows the combination beats strong baselines under adversarial conditions. The holdout result (Section 7) shows a meaningful but narrow advantage on high-risk decisions only.

## 3. Formal model

See `research/EVIDENCE_INTEGRITY_FORMAL_MODEL.md`. Key rules:

- **E1 (common-cause discount):** one ancestry cluster contributes at most one independent unit of support, regardless of how many copies agree.
- **E2 (unknown-provenance cap):** unknown-provenance evidence is pooled into a single weak cluster with reliability capped at 0.5; it never acts as independent support.
- **E3 (scope gating):** assurance is aggregated per required subclaim; the claim confidence is the weakest link (min over subclaims).
- Decision thresholds are risk-dependent: accept ≥ {0.85, 0.92, 0.98}, reject ≤ {0.15, 0.08, 0.03} for {low, med, high}.
- **Unknown independence must not silently become assumed independence** (core requirement).

## 4. Selected mechanism

**Candidate E — Evidence Integrity and Decision Gate, narrowed to a high-risk veto.**

The full "Evidence Integrity Engine" (provenance-aware aggregation over all evidence) is **too conservative**: it abstains 71–74% of the time and has lower coverage than a strong verifier. The valuable subset is a **high-risk acceptance veto**: trust the strong verifier on low/medium-risk tasks (full coverage), but require provenance-aware sufficiency (≥ 0.98 confidence) before accepting on high-risk tasks. On high risk, the gate acquires the deterministic rule, the cheap judge, the external registry, and the strong judge, clusters them by ancestry, and abstains (INDETERMINATE) when the supporting evidence is not independent enough.

Rejected candidates (see `research/FAILED_HYPOTHESES.md`):
- Candidate A (Evidence Dependency Graph alone) — no decision advantage without the gate.
- Candidate B (Provenance-Aware Confidence alone) — safe but too conservative as a general policy.
- Candidate C (Contradiction-Driven Investigation) — very cheap (cost 0.63) but very low coverage (8–18%).
- Candidate D (Claim-Level Evidence Sufficiency alone) — scope gating is necessary but not sufficient; must be combined with provenance discounting.

## 5. Experimental design

Simulator: `experiments/benchmark/evidence_integrity.py` (isolated from production runtime).

- Task: a claim with **2 required subclaims** (numeric + factual) plus one out-of-scope property (format). Claim true iff both subclaims are true.
- Evidence channels: `det_rule` (deterministic, scope = numeric, applicable 45%, always correct), `cheap_judge` (scope both, accuracy 0.85/0.15 by family quality), `strong_judge` (scope both, accuracy 0.95/0.05, shares family with cheap w.p. 0.7), `ext_registry` (scope factual, accuracy 0.85, independent), `format_check` (out of scope, always passes, reliability 0.6), `duplicate` (exact copy of cheap judge).
- Hidden family quality: 70% good, 30% bad (common-cause failure). Ancestry metadata dropped w.p. 0.3 (unknown provenance). False provenance (forged metadata) w.p. 0–1 depending on condition.
- Policies never see difficulty, ground truth, family quality, or subclaim correctness. Calibration uses labels on a separate split only.
- Seeds: development {42, 123, 456}; holdout {2024, 2025, 2026, 2027, 2028}. Held-out task IDs disjoint from calibration by construction.
- Randomness: SHA-256-based seeds (never Python `hash()`); cross-process deterministic (verified).

Dataset composition (per seed): 200 calibration + 500 evaluation tasks. Risk mix ≈ 50% low / 30% med / 20% high. Claim truth rate ≈ 0.59.

## 7. Main results (holdout)

- **strong_high_risk_gate** reduces mean risk-weighted loss from **0.699** (single_strong) to **0.518**, a **25.9% reduction**, at coverage **81.3%** and cost **2.20** (10% higher).
- Decision accuracy among decided tasks: 0.610 / 0.813 = **0.750**, vs single_strong 0.741 → matched (within 1pp).
- Pooled FAR: **5.88%** vs single_strong 7.63% → 22.9% relative reduction.
- Pooled FRR: 29.4% vs 37.5% (gate improves both).
- The gate is the only policy that simultaneously achieves ≥80% coverage, FAR below the strong verifier, and risk-weighted loss below the strong verifier.

## 8. Uncertainty estimates

- Wilson 95% CI for FAR: gate [4.56%, 7.54%] vs single_strong [6.12%, 9.47%] — intervals overlap slightly, but the loss difference is consistent across all 5 seeds (gate loss < single_strong loss in every seed).
- Per-seed gate loss: 517.6 ± 28 (mean ± 1 SE across 5 seeds). Per-seed single_strong: 698.8 ± 33. The 181-point difference is ~4.5 SE, statistically significant.
- Success rates: gate 0.610 [0.570, 0.642], single_strong 0.741 [0.716, 0.762].

## 18. Research decision

**NARROW.**

The narrow gate (provenance-aware sufficiency for high-risk accepts only) demonstrates a meaningful, reproducible advantage: 25.9% risk-weighted loss reduction, 22.9% FAR reduction, at matched decision quality and 81% coverage, with graceful degradation under distribution shift, correlated failures, and unknown provenance. The broader "Evidence Integrity Engine" as a general decision policy remains too conservative (71–74% abstention). The valuable, deployable subset is the high-risk veto.

## 19. Biggest unresolved scientific question

Does the provenance-aware veto still outperform a strong verifier when the verifiers are **real models** with measured, non-simulated error correlation? The simulation assumes a specific common-cause structure (shared family quality); real LLM judges may have more complex, less structured correlations that the cluster model cannot capture. A 10–20-task live probe (≤ $0.05) would measure the actual judge correlation structure, but it cannot establish reliable rates. The next major question is whether **authenticated provenance metadata** (signed source lineage) is feasible for AI execution systems, which would close the false-provenance vulnerability.

## 20. Recommended next major invention cycle

Cycle 4 — **Live Verification of the High-Risk Gate**: implement the gate as a minimal, budget-gated runtime component; run the capped live probe (≤ $0.05, 10–20 tasks) to measure real judge FPR/FNR and error correlation with the generator; decide whether to promote the gate to a production feature or abandon the provenance-tracking layer. Do not build the full Evidence Integrity Engine until the live correlation structure is known.
