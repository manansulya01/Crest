## 2. Design under test

- Candidate grid: 3 actions x 2 families x 7 recoveries = 42 candidates.
- Evaluation: 42 candidates x 54 trials = 2,268 trials.
  - 3 replicate bases: `200000`, `300000`, `400000`.
  - 6 distributions: `IN_DISTRIBUTION`, `PARAMETER_SHIFT`, `STRUCTURAL_SHIFT`,
    `COMPOSITION_SHIFT`, `ADVERSARIAL_SHIFT`, `OUT_OF_FAMILY`.
  - 3 seeds per distribution per replicate.
- Policy sample size: n=12 training, n=12 holdout, n=6 per shift.
- Experimental failure bound: `0.20`.
- Policy seeds: verification `1000`, holdout `90000`, shifts `50000`.
- Evaluation seeds are disjoint from policy seeds.
- Deployable policies: `deny_all`, `admit_all`, `fixture_only`, `family_only`,
  `generalized`.
- Diagnostic only: `oracle`, using independent evaluation labels after policy
  decisions. It is not deployable and was not imported by the policy module.

## 5. Important negative and limiting findings

1. **Fixture-only evidence is unsafe on this grid.**
   Six of sixteen fixture-only admissions were unsafe (37.5%). This supports
   the narrower V0.4 claim that graded evidence reduces unsafe admissions
   relative to fixture-only evidence on unseen environments.

2. **The safety comparison is small.**
   The paired test has only six discordant candidate pairs. An exact two-sided
   p-value of 0.03125 is not strong evidence of a broad effect, and the
   one-sided p-value is 0.015625. No multiplicity adjustment beyond the plan
   was applied. The result should be described as a measured 6/16 versus 0/10
   unsafe-admission reduction, not as a general safety guarantee.

3. **Family-only and generalized admit the same ten candidates here.**
   The generalized policy adds shift-distribution evidence, but on this
   candidate grid it does not admit a different set than family-only. The
   independent validation does not demonstrate an admission-count advantage of
   generalized over family-only; it demonstrates precision and shift evidence.

4. **`admit_all` exposes the risk in the grid.**
   It admitted 42 candidates, 32 of them unsafe, with precision 0.238095.
   `deny_all` avoided unsafe admissions but rejected all 10 reliable
   candidates. The grid contains both hazards, so the PASS is not an artifact
   of a trivial candidate set.

5. **The experimental policy is not the production policy.**
   The validation uses n=12 and a 0.20 failure bound. At n=12 with zero
   failures, the one-sided Wilson bound is approximately 0.184, below 0.20.
   Production defaults (family 0.05, general 0.01, minima 20/50) would not
   grant the same classes at this sample size. Any V0.5 runtime claim must
   state that production evidence requires the production policy and its
   larger sample minima.

6. **Drift evidence is weaker than the V0.4 labels imply.**
   The canonical drift table records:
   - `target_moved`: 0/3 detected, labelled `implemented`.
   - `target_renamed`: 0/3 detected, labelled `implemented`.
   - `directory_structure_changed`: 3/3 detected.
   - Implemented-class detection rate: 0.3333.
   - Five drift classes (`additional_distractors`, `file_content_changed`,
     `git_state_changed`, `scope_changed`, `symlink_introduced`) are labelled
     `unimplemented`.
   This is a real contradiction in the committed V0.4 evidence. It does not
   invalidate the independent recovery-admission result, but it does mean the
   V0.5 runtime must not claim drift coverage beyond directory-structure
   change until the pinned-target move/rename path is investigated. Runtime
   pinned-target tests exercise a different path than the canonical drift
   experiment.

7. **Confidence invariance is decision invariance, not calibration.**
   The canonical confidence-sensitivity table has 16 identical admission rows
   across 0.90/0.95/0.99/0.999. Honest recovery stays `VERIFIED_FIXTURE` at
   all levels; the overfit recovery stays `UNVERIFIED`. At n=6, no candidate
   reaches family or general evidence. The sweep demonstrates that the
   confidence parameter changes the bound monotonically, not that it is a
   calibrated real-world probability.

8. **No live provider was tested.**
   Phase B must use deterministic fake adapters and must mark any live-provider
   adapter as unverified. The validation result provides no evidence about
   model inference.

9. **Independence is code and seed separation, not external validation.**
   The validation generator, policies, and oracle are authored in the same
   repository. Their independence comes from module boundaries, disjoint seed
   bases, a third generator, and isolated evaluation labels. This is stronger
   than rerunning the same seeds, but it is not independent replication by a
   separate team.

## 7. Stage gate decision

Phase A status: **PASS**.

The preregistered claim is supported on the measured synthetic grid:
graded recovery evidence reduced unsafe admissions from 6/16 to 0/10, retained
10/10 reliable candidates, and achieved precision 1.0 for both family-only and
generalized evidence. The exact paired p-value is 0.03125, and the sample is
small, so the claim must remain scoped to this candidate grid and to synthetic
filesystem recovery.

Phase B may proceed, but with the following narrowed claims:
- V0.4 recovery evidence validates a tool-capability admission policy, not a
  model-inference runtime.
- Model inference requires a separate adapter, budget, token ledger, policy,
  and verification path.
- Production evidence claims require the production policy and its larger
  sample minima; the n=12 experimental result is not production evidence.
- Drift claims are limited to directory-structure change until target move and
  rename detection is corrected.
- No live-provider claim may be made without actual provider calls.
