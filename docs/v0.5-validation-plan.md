# Crest V0.5 Independent Validation Plan

Status: **prospective preregistration**. This plan is committed before the
confirmatory validation run. Exploratory smoke runs at `n <= 2` may be used to
debug the harness; they are excluded from the confirmatory result. The
SHA-256 of this file at the validation commit is recorded in the result
artifact.

Starting baseline: `042ed7b703e7ffa3165d99136bfb262f64630b77`
(`test: close canonical reproduction release gate`).

## Research question

The V0.4 claim under test is:

> Graded recovery evidence reduces unsafe capability admissions relative to
> fixture-only evidence, on environments that were not used to produce the
> evidence, while retaining useful reliable coverage.

This is narrower than “CREST is safe” or “CREST reduces model inference
cost.” The experiment tests the recovery-admission policy, not model routing
or provider economics.

## Hypotheses and primary outcomes

### H1 — Safety (primary)

On a fresh candidate set, the fixture-only policy will produce at least one
unsafe admission, and the generalized policy will produce fewer unsafe
admissions than fixture-only (target: zero).

- Unit: one (action, family, recovery) candidate.
- Unsafe admission: `admitted and not reliable`.
- Reliable: every independent evaluation trial passes (54/54 across three
  evaluation seed replicates).
- Primary estimate: raw unsafe-admission count and rate among admitted
  candidates, plus rate among all candidates.

### H2 — Reliable coverage (primary)

The generalized policy will admit at least 8 of the 12 genuinely reliable
candidates and will not reject a reliable candidate (zero false rejections).

- False rejection: `not admitted and reliable`.
- Coverage is reported separately from raw admission coverage: admitting
  unsafe candidates does not count as useful coverage.

### H3 — Evidence precision (primary)

Every candidate admitted by the family-only or generalized policy will be
reliable (precision 1.0). If a candidate is admitted with independent success
below 1.0, the claim is narrowed and the failure is reported.

## Secondary outcomes (descriptive, not gate-changing)

- Verification wall-clock seconds by policy, and generalized/fixture ratio.
- Decision invariance across confidence levels 0.90/0.95/0.99/0.999 at n=6.
- Drift scenarios: detector output versus runtime revocation, true pinned
  rename/move, and post-revocation execution outcome.
- Digest mutation matrix: timing/timestamp/machine changes preserve
  `result_digest`; parameter/classification/count/content changes alter it.
- Adversarial harness cases: empty candidate set, duplicate IDs, missing
  outcomes, corrupted inputs, malformed confidence values, verifier failure,
  and evaluation-label leakage.

Secondary outcomes are reported with the same prominence as negative
findings. They do not alter H1–H3 thresholds after the run.

## Baselines and information access

| Policy | Information access | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| `deny_all` | None | Never admit |
| `admit_all` | None | Always admit |
| `fixture_only` | Training-generator trials only, n=12 | Admit iff 12/12 pass |
| `family_only` | Training n=12 + independent holdout n=12; scaled policy bound 0.20 | Admit iff classified at least `VERIFIED_FAMILY` |
| `generalized` | Training n=12 + holdout n=12 + three shifts at n=6; scaled policy bound 0.20 | Admit iff classified `VERIFIED_GENERAL` |
| `oracle` | Independent evaluation labels | Admit iff reliable; **diagnostic only, not deployable** |

All deployable policies receive only the candidate identity, the verifier
generators/seeds permitted to them, and the declared policy. No deployable
policy receives the validation generator, evaluation seeds, evaluation
labels, or the oracle result. The evaluation oracle is implemented in a
separate module and is not imported by the policy module.

The policies use the **experimental scaled policy** documented in
`experiments/configs/experiment.yaml` (failure bound 0.20, sample minima
scaled to n). This is not the production policy. At n=12 and zero failures,
the one-sided Wilson bound is approximately 0.184, below 0.20. Production
defaults (family bound 0.05, general bound 0.01, minima 20/50) would not grant
the same classes at this sample size; that difference is reported, not hidden.

## Data and fixture construction

Candidate grid:

- Actions: `delete_file`, `modify_file`, `move_file`.
- Families: `filesystem.single_file`, `filesystem.directory_tree`.
- Recoveries: `restore_from_snapshot`, `restore_file_from_snapshot`,
  `overfit_distractor_gate`, `validation_overfit_training_shape` (registered
  only by the validation harness), `noop_fault`, `restore_truncated`,
  `restore_with_side_effect`.
- Total: 3 × 2 × 7 = 42 candidates.

The validation-only overfit recovery restores state only when a
training-generator marker is present; it is a deliberately adversarial
candidate, not a production recovery program. It is declared here before
the run.

Evaluation environments use a **third generator** implemented in
`experiments/validation/generator.py`. It is not imported by the policy
module or by `crest.generalization`. It constructs deterministic flat or
nested trees from a seeded RNG, independent of both `crest.fixtures` and
`crest.holdout_gen`.

Evaluation distributions: `IN_DISTRIBUTION`, `PARAMETER_SHIFT`,
`STRUCTURAL_SHIFT`, `COMPOSITION_SHIFT`, `ADVERSARIAL_SHIFT`,
`OUT_OF_FAMILY` (six distributions, using `crest.distributions` on top of the
third generator).

Evaluation seeds: three replicate bases `200000`, `300000`, `400000`, with
one deterministic seed per (candidate, distribution, replicate). Each
candidate therefore has 6 × 3 = 18 evaluation trials per replicate and 54
trials total. A trial error or exception counts as a failure and is recorded.

Policy verification seeds are the existing fixed bases: verification `1000`,
holdout `90000`, shifts `50000`. They are disjoint from the evaluation bases.

## Sample sizes and computational plan

- Policy sample size: n=12 for training and holdout; n=6 per shift
  distribution for the generalized policy.
- Evaluation: 42 candidates × 54 trials = 2,268 trials.
- Expected policy verification work: approximately 42 × (12 + 24 + 42) =
  3,276 synthetic trials, plus evaluation. Expected wall-clock: a few
  minutes on the development machine; timings are not a scientific outcome.
- Canonical reproduction: run the documented `crest research reproduce
  --full` at the committed validation revision, in a clean detached
  worktree, at least twice if computationally feasible. Record commit,
  branch, dirty state, config digest, manifest path, result digest, and
  table hashes. Do not rewrite the historical V0.4 manifests.

## Statistical analysis

- Report raw counts and denominators for every policy and outcome.
- Use Wilson score 95% intervals for proportions; do not apply a normal
  approximation blindly.
- For the paired H1 comparison, use the exact McNemar/binomial sign test on
  discordant candidate pairs (fixture-only unsafe vs generalized unsafe).
  Report the two-sided p-value and the one-sided safety alternative.
- Report per-distribution and per-replicate descriptive tables.
- Do not claim statistical significance for small counts merely because a
  rate looks favorable. Do not claim equivalence from a non-significant
  result.
- No candidates are excluded. Missing or failed trials count as failures.
  If the harness cannot produce a valid result artifact, the outcome is
  `INCONCLUSIVE`, not a silent pass.

## Decision rules

### H1

- `PASS`: fixture-only unsafe count > 0, generalized unsafe count = 0, and
  generalized unsafe count < fixture-only unsafe count.
- `PARTIAL`: generalized unsafe count < fixture-only unsafe count, but
  generalized unsafe count > 0.
- `FAIL`: generalized unsafe count >= fixture-only unsafe count, or
  fixture-only unsafe count = 0.
- `INCONCLUSIVE`: the harness could not evaluate the candidate set.

### H2

- `PASS`: generalized admits all reliable candidates (zero false rejections)
  and reliable coverage is at least 8/12.
- `PARTIAL`: reliable coverage is at least 8/12 but at least one reliable
  candidate is rejected.
- `FAIL`: reliable coverage is below 8/12.

### H3

- `PASS`: family-only and generalized precision = 1.0.
- `PARTIAL`: precision >= 0.90 for both.
- `FAIL`: precision < 0.90 for either.

### Overall scientific decision

- `PASS`: H1, H2, and H3 all PASS.
- `PARTIAL`: H1 PASS and neither H2 nor H3 FAIL.
- `FAIL`: H1 FAIL.
- `INCONCLUSIVE`: the harness or canonical reproduction cannot produce
  valid evidence.

A `PARTIAL` result must narrow the runtime claims to the measured capability.
A `FAIL` result must not be converted into a product claim.
